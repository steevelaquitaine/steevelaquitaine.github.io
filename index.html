AI Research Scientist & Computational Neuroscientist
Sorbonne Université · Paris Vision Institute
Stanford University Affiliate · Formerly EPFL

I study how biological and artificial neural networks represent visual information, learn its statistical structure, and use it for efficient inference and decision-making.

Research

My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computational neuroscience, and statistical inference. I develop mathematical and machine-learning models to uncover the computational principles that enable intelligent systems to make flexible, robust decisions under uncertainty and limited resources.

I connect these models with large-scale neural recordings and behavioral experiments, using insights from biological intelligence to inform the design and interpretation of artificial neural networks.

Current focus: How do biological and artificial neural networks encode visual inputs and their statistical structure to support efficient inference?

Background: I am currently a postdoctoral researcher in Matthew Chalk’s lab at the Paris Vision Institute. Previously, I held postdoctoral positions with Henry Markram and Michael Reimann at the Blue Brain Project, EPFL, and with Justin Gardner at Stanford University. I also worked in industry as a Lead Data Scientist, developing and deploying scalable machine-learning systems.
